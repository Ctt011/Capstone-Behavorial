A motion-aware, duration-aware stress system that turns HR/HRV signals into an intuitive "Body Battery" energy percentage, then recommends recovery actions and validates improvement through HealthKit measurements.

Pipeline at a glance

Stage 1 filters movement vs. stillness using a Random Forest classifier trained on 22 subjects — preventing exercise from being mislabeled as stress.

Stage 2 adjusts daily sensitivity using sleep-based rules — poor sleep lowers your stress threshold.

Stage 3 computes a personalized HRV-based stress score (0–100) using DC/AC metrics and personal baselines, driving Body Battery drain and recovery guidance.

Why It Matters

Problem. Approach. Impact.

01

The Problem

Stress signals are subtle, highly individual, and difficult to learn from small cohorts. Pure ML approaches fail on limited data — published classifiers achieve only F1=56% for stress vs. neutral. A fundamentally different approach is needed.

02

Our Approach

A 3-stage hybrid pipeline for Apple Watch data. Stage 1 uses ML to classify physical vs. cognitive states (93.6% accuracy), preventing exercise from being mislabeled as stress. Stage 2 applies sleep-based rules. Stage 3 computes an HRV stress score using DC/AC metrics and personal baselines — not population averages.

03

The Impact

The Body Battery gives users a simple energy percentage they instantly understand. It drains based on stress intensity and duration, then recommends recovery actions. During recovery, the system measures changes via HealthKit to validate improvement — closing the loop from Detect → Quantify → Intervene → Validate → Learn.

Last night's sleep quality adjusts your stress sensitivity threshold. Poor sleep means your body is more vulnerable — we account for that.

Duration + quality → threshold shift

Stress Scoring

Signal Processing

DC/AC from PRSA, SDNN, and RMSSD computed from RR intervals, compared against your personal baseline. Not a population average — yours.

Output

Body Battery

Your stress score drains the battery throughout the day. Exercise drains it too — but isn't counted as stress. Recovery and calm periods stabilize it.

Honest Science

What we found — and what we didn't.

DC/AC values were not statistically significant on the WISE dataset for single-snapshot REST vs. STRESS classification (p > 0.05). This is consistent with published findings — Bahameish et al. achieved only F1=56% for Stress vs. Neutral. This is exactly why we use personal baselines and a hybrid approach instead of one-shot classification.

Training Data

WISE Dataset
